What is Liver Cirrhosis

Liver cirrhosis is irreversible scarring caused by chronic liver damage (hepatitis, alcohol, NAFLD). It progresses in two stages:

  • Compensated Cirrhosis: Asymptomatic; managed with lifestyle changes and monitoring.
  • Decompensated Cirrhosis: Life-threatening complications like ascites, variceal bleeding, or hepatic encephalopathy.

Cirrhosis Complications & Management

1. Ascites (Fluid Retention)

  • Treatment:
    • Diuretics: Spironolactone + furosemide.
    • Albumin Infusion: Restores blood volume and reduces fluid buildup.
    • Paracentesis: Fluid drainage for severe cases.

2. Variceal Bleeding

  • Prevention: Non-selective beta-blockers (propranolol).
  • Emergency Care: Endoscopic band ligation or sclerotherapy.

3. Hepatic Encephalopathy

  • Treatment: Lactulose (reduces ammonia) + rifaximin.
  • Monitoring: Regular neuro assessments and blood tests.

4. Portal Hypertension

TIPS Procedure: Minimally invasive shunt to reduce vein pressure (success rate: 80%).

Cirrhosis Reversal Strategies

  • Early-Stage Reversal:
    • Alcohol Cessation: Stops further damage.
    • Antivirals: Cure hepatitis B/C to halt fibrosis.
    • Weight Loss: For NAFLD-related cirrhosis.
  • Advanced Care:

Liver Transplant: For MELD scores ≥15 or recurrent complications.

Managing Sarcopenia (Muscle Loss)

  • High-Protein Diet: 1.2–1.5g/kg/day with plant/lean animal proteins.
  • Resistance Training: Prevents muscle wasting.

Supplements: BCAA (branched-chain amino acids) to combat fatigue.
