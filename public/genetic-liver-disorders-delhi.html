What Are Genetic Liver Disorders?

Genetic liver disorders are inherited conditions causing abnormal liver function due to gene mutations. Key conditions we treat

  • Hemochromatosis: Iron overload damages the liver, heart, and joints
  • Wilson’s Disease: Toxic copper accumulation leading to cirrhosis and neurological issues.
  • Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency: Misfolded proteins causing liver and lung damage.
  • Pediatric Metabolic Disorders: Glycogen storage diseases, tyrosinemia, and galactosemia.

Early diagnosis and targeted therapy can prevent irreversible damage.

Diagnosis of Genetic Liver Disorders

1. Genetic Testing

  • Panels: Screen for mutations in HFE (hemochromatosis), ATP7B (Wilson’s), and SERPINA1 (Alpha-1).
  • Pediatric Testing: Rapid whole-exome sequencing for metabolic diseases like tyrosinemia.

2. Blood & Imaging

  • Iron Studies: Transferrin saturation, ferritin (hemochromatosis).
  • Ceruloplasmin: Low levels indicate Wilson’s disease.

Liver Elastography: Assess fibrosis in Alpha-1 patients.

Treatment Options

1. Hemochromatosis Treatment

  • Phlebotomy: Weekly blood removal (500 mL) to reduce iron.
  • Iron Chelation: Deferasirox for patients unable to undergo phlebotomy.

2. Wilson’s Disease Management

  • Copper Chelation Therapy: Penicillamine or trientine to remove excess copper.
  • Zinc Acetate: Blocks intestinal copper absorption.
  • Low-Copper Diet: Avoid shellfish, nuts, and chocolate.

3. Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency

  • Enzyme Replacement: Augmentation therapy for lung involvement.
  • Liver Transplant: For cirrhosis or HCC.

4. Pediatric Metabolic Liver Disease

  • Dietary Therapy: Galactose-free diets for galactosemia.

Nitisinone: For tyrosinemia to block toxic metabolite production.
