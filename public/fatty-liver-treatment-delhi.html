What is Fatty Liver Disease?

Fatty liver disease happens when excess fat builds up in liver cells, categorized into

Key Risk Groups

  • MASLD (Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease): Linked to obesity, diabetes, or metabolic syndrome.
  • NASH (Non-Alcoholic Steatohepatitis): A severe form causing inflammation and fibrosis.
  • Left untreated, it can progress to advanced fibrosis, cirrhosis, or liver failure.

Causes & Symptoms

  • Key Causes:
    • Obesity: Primary driver of MASLD (60–80% of cases).
    • Insulin resistance, high cholesterol, and sedentary lifestyle.

Symptoms: Fatigue, abdominal discomfort, and jaundice (in advanced stages).

Advanced Diagnosis

  • MASLD/NASH Diagnosis:
    • FibroScan®: Non-invasive staging of liver stiffness and fat content.
    • MRI Elastography: Detects early fibrosis.
    • Liver Biopsy for MASH: Confirms inflammation and fibrosis severity.

Blood Tests: ALT/AST ratios, lipid profiles, and HbA1c.

Fatty Liver Disease Treatment

1. Reversing Fatty Liver Damage

  • Weight Loss: 5–10% reduction reverses early-stage MASLD.
  • Medications: Vitamin E, pioglitazone (for NASH), and GLP-1 agonists (for obesity-linked cases).

2. NASH Cirrhosis Management

  • Lifestyle Changes: Low-carb diets, aerobic exercise, and alcohol avoidance.
  • Monitoring: Regular FibroScan® to track fibrosis progression.
  • Transplant Evaluation: For decompensated cirrhosis (MELD score ≥15).

3. Lifestyle Interventions

  • Diet: A Mediterranean diet rich in antioxidants.

Exercise: 150+ minutes/week of moderate activity.
