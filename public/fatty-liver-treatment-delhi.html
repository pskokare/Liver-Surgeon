What Is Fatty Liver Disease?

Fatty liver disease occurs when excess fat accumulates in liver cells, impairing the liver's ability to function normally. It has become one of the most common liver conditions in India, affecting an estimated 1 in 3 urban adults — most of whom have no symptoms until the disease has already progressed.

Main Types of Fatty Liver Disease

  • Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD/MASLD): The most common type in India, linked to obesity, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome.
  • Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (AFLD): Caused by excessive and long-term alcohol consumption.

The encouraging news is that when diagnosed early, fatty liver disease is highly treatable — and in many cases, completely reversible. The key is expert, timely intervention.

Fatty Liver Disease: Stages & Progression

Fatty liver disease is not a single condition — it progresses through four stages, each requiring a different level of care. Identifying your stage early is critical to achieving the best outcome.

Stage Condition Outlook Action
Stage 1 Simple Fat Accumulation (Steatosis) Fully Reversible Lifestyle & medical management
Stage 2 Liver Inflammation (NASH/MASH) Largely Reversible Lifestyle & medical management
Stage 3 Liver Fibrosis (Scarring) Partially Manageable Specialist care required
Stage 4 Liver Cirrhosis Not Reversible Transplant evaluation needed

Symptoms of Fatty Liver Disease

Fatty liver disease is often called the "silent disease" because most patients have no symptoms in the early stages. This is why proactive screening is so important — especially if you have risk factors.

Early Stage Symptoms

  • None in most cases
  • Some may notice:
  • Mild fatigue or low energy
  • Vague upper right abdominal discomfort
  • Nausea after oily meals

Advanced Stage Symptoms

Seek immediate specialist care if you notice:

  • Jaundice (yellow skin or eyes)
  • Abdominal swelling or bloating
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Persistent fatigue and weakness
  • Leg swelling or easy bruising

Who Is at Risk? Common Causes in India

Fatty liver disease is closely linked to modern Indian lifestyle patterns. You may be at higher risk if you:

  • Are overweight or obese, especially with excess belly fat
  • Have type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance
  • Follow a diet high in refined carbohydrates, sugar, or fried foods (maida, mithai, packaged snacks)
  • Lead a sedentary lifestyle with little physical activity
  • Have high cholesterol or triglycerides
  • Consume alcohol regularly
  • Have a family history of liver disease or metabolic conditions
  • Have PCOS, hypothyroidism, or other metabolic disorders

Even people who are not overweight can develop fatty liver — a condition known as "lean NAFLD," which is more prevalent in South Asia than in Western populations.

How We Diagnose Fatty Liver at Liversurgeons.com

Accurate diagnosis and staging are the foundation of effective treatment. Our team uses a comprehensive, multi-modal diagnostic approach:

  • Liver Function Tests (LFTs)
    Blood tests that measure liver enzyme levels (ALT, AST, GGT) to detect inflammation and liver stress. These are typically the first indicator of a liver problem.
  • Abdominal Ultrasound
    A non-invasive imaging test that detects fat deposits in the liver. Quick, painless, and widely available — and often the first test to flag fatty liver.
  • FibroScan & Liver Elastography
    A specialised, needle-free test that measures liver stiffness to accurately assess the degree of fibrosis (scarring). This is one of the most valuable tools for staging fatty liver without invasive procedures.
  • Advanced MRI / CT Imaging
    MRI-PDFF precisely quantifies liver fat percentage and is used in complex or advanced cases where detailed imaging is needed.
  • Liver Biopsy
    The gold standard for definitive diagnosis, particularly for distinguishing between simple steatosis and NASH/MASH. Recommended when non-invasive tests are inconclusive or when staging accuracy is critical.
