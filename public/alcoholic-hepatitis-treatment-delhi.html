Understanding Autoimmune Hepatitis

Autoimmune hepatitis is often misunderstood. It is not caused by a virus, as with viral hepatitis, and it is not due to alcohol consumption or anything you might have eaten recently.

This condition arises because your immune system—which is designed to protect you from infections—loses its ability to tell the difference between your own cells (“self”) and foreign invaders. Instead of attacking bacteria or viruses, it mistakenly targets your liver cells.

The result is chronic inflammation in the liver. While your liver is resilient and tries to heal itself, ongoing inflammation eventually leads to scarring. If left unchecked, this scarring can progress to fibrosis and even cirrhosis, seriously impacting liver function.

There are two primary types of autoimmune hepatitis:

  • Type 1 AIH: The most common type, usually affecting adolescents and adults.
  • Type 2 AIH: Rarer and seen more often in children and young adults.

Though anyone can develop autoimmune hepatitis, many of my patients in Delhi are women, especially those between 15-40 or 50-60 years of age. In older age groups, men sometimes experience a more rapid and severe progression of the disease.

What triggers this abnormal immune response? The exact cause remains unclear. Genetics or environmental factors may play a role, but one thing is certain: autoimmune hepatitis is a chronic liver condition that requires careful medical management.

Common Symptoms Include:

  • Persistent fatigue
  • Jaundice (yellowing of skin and eyes)
  • Abdominal discomfort
  • Loss of appetite
  • Dark-colored urine
  • Joint pain

In some cases, there may be no clear symptoms until liver damage has become significant. That’s why early consultation with an AIH specialist in Delhi is so important for timely diagnosis and effective management.

Diagnosis of Autoimmune Hepatitis

The biggest trap with autoimmune hepatitis is believing that “I feel okay, so it cannot be serious.”

Why can symptoms be misleading? Symptoms are signals from the body indicating that something is wrong, but in many cases, they do not act as a precise diagnostic code. They resemble other forms of chronic liver disease. Therefore, careful diagnosis is needed for effective autoimmune hepatitis treatment.

Blood Tests

Diagnosis is careful, layered, and systematic. Specialized autoantibody tests help detect immune system activity against the liver. These include:

  • Antinuclear antibodies (ANA)
  • Anti–liver kidney microsomal antibodies (anti-LKM)
  • Anti-smooth muscle antibodies (ASMA)

These markers help indicate that the immune system is involved. But they are not the entire story.

Liver Function Tests (LFTs): Measuring Damage

LFTs measure liver enzymes such as ALT and AST. Elevated levels indicate liver inflammation or damage. Monitoring these enzymes helps us to assess bilirubin and protein levels to evaluate liver performance.

Liver Biopsy: The Deciding Factor

In many cases, a liver biopsy is recommended. This is not done casually. It gives us direct information about:

  • The degree of inflammation
  • The stage of fibrosis
  • Whether cirrhosis has begun

The biopsy often confirms both the diagnosis and the severity, allowing us to tailor autoimmune hepatitis treatment in Delhi precisely to the patient’s condition.

Treatment Options for Autoimmune Hepatitis

Now comes the most important question: can autoimmune hepatitis be treated?

Yes. And often very effectively.

The foundation of treatment is immunosuppressive therapy. The goal is simple but critical: calm down the immune system before it causes permanent damage.

Immunosuppressive Therapy

Treatment typically begins with corticosteroids such as prednisone to reduce liver inflammation. Many patients see their liver enzyme levels improve within weeks. But steroids alone are not a long-term solution. Prolonged high doses can cause side effects.

In many cases, another medication like azathioprine is added to suppress the immune response more effectively and allow for lower steroid doses. Together, these medicines form the backbone of autoimmune hepatitis treatment worldwide. This combination helps minimize long-term side effects while maintaining disease control.

Immunosuppressive therapy is carefully monitored by liver specialists like us. Regular follow-ups and blood tests ensure that medication doses are adjusted appropriately.

Managing Flare-Ups

Autoimmune hepatitis can sometimes flare up, causing a sudden increase in symptoms or liver enzyme levels.

This does not mean treatment has failed. It means adjustments are required. Medication doses may be increased temporarily, and close monitoring ensures inflammation is brought back under control.

Long-Term Outcomes

While AIH cannot always be completely cured, what are we trying to achieve?

The goal is remission.

Many patients achieve long-term remission with consistent treatment. Early diagnosis and adherence to therapy significantly improve prognosis. The goal is to preserve liver function, prevent complications, and maintain a good quality of life.
